2. WHERE THE SITE IS NOW · AUDIT OVERVIEW

Before writing this we ran a full audit of 10xconcierge.com: search visibility, technical health, backlink profile, page structure and speed. Everything below is measured, not guessed, and every recommendation in this document comes from these findings.
The Headline Gap

The headline: the site currently has 192 pages carrying 914 technical issues between them. That's the gap in one line. A site this size should be an asset. Right now the volume of pages and problems is working against you, not for you, and the rest of this section is where those 914 issues come from.

Your site is effectively invisible in search

• Your domain authority score is 2 out of 100.
• The site currently ranks in the top 30 positions for zero keywords.
• The handful of terms it does appear for sit between positions 31 and 100, which is page four and beyond, where almost nobody looks.
• Worse, those terms are the wrong ones. The site's best rankings are for "aircraft concierge service" at position 98 and "private jet concierge" at position 77. 100 percent of the keywords you rank for are informational, not booking intent. Nothing you rank for is a term someone types when they're ready to book a transfer.

Section 2 (Continued)

2. HOMEPAGE TECHNICAL DIAGNOSTICS

The homepage is not telling Google what you do
Title Tag Signal

Your homepage title tag reads Home - 10X CONCIERGE. Twenty characters, and not one of them is a service or a location. This is the single strongest signal Google reads, and right now it says nothing about private chauffeur transfers in Milan, Malpensa, Como or Lugano.

Meta Description Signal

Your meta description is 6,691 characters long. It should be between 120 and 160. What's there now is the entire page text dumped into the field, including customer review quotes. Google won't use it, so your search result shows a truncated fragment instead of a reason to click.

Word Count Signal

Meanwhile the homepage itself has a word count of 278, which search engines classify as thin content. So the description is 24 times too long and the page itself is too short. Both are fixable in the rebuild.

H1 Tag Signal

The page also carries more than one H1 tag, which splits the signal about what the page is even about.

The content is pulling in the wrong direction

Section 2 (Continued)

2. BACKLINKS, LOCAL & STRENGTHS

Your backlink profile needs attention

You have 297 backlinks from 127 referring domains, but the quality is the problem. Only 12 are dofollow. The referring domains are concentrated in Finland, France, Singapore and Colombia, not Italy or Switzerland, and one anchor text appearing across 13 domains openly advertises a paid link network. This is the signature of low-quality or purchased links, and it can actively hold a site back rather than help it. It needs a proper audit and, where appropriate, a disavow.

Missing Local & Multilingual Foundations

• No LocalBusiness schema on the site, despite having a Google Business Profile in Stabio with a 5 star rating and 10 reviews. Google isn't connecting the two.
• No hreflang tags. Your clients already search in English, Italian and German. Site currently declares itself as en-US only.
• Zero AI search visibility. No mentions in ChatGPT, Gemini or Google AI Overviews.

The One Genuine Strength

Usability scores A+. The site works properly across devices, which means the foundation is sound and this is a fixable situation rather than a rebuild from zero.

Section 3

3. THE APPROACH · PRINCIPLES 1 & 2

Four principles drive every recommendation in this document.
1. Fewer, stronger pages beat many thin ones

For Google Ads, sending a campaign to a tightly matched page makes sense and lowers your cost per click. But for organic search, a separate page for every single route works against you. Ten near-identical pages split your ranking strength ten ways and compete with each other. We give the priority routes, Milan to Como and Como to Lugano, their own strong dedicated pages, and group the rest until search demand justifies pulling them out.

2. Topical authority built around Como first

Google ranks a site higher when it sees that site as a real authority on a subject. Your site is the clearest possible example of the problem: the pages cover Greek villas and Italian food, while terms you rank for are about private jets. Google has no idea you're the transfer specialist for your corridor. So we concentrate everything on Como as the hub, the Milan to Como and Como to Lugano routes as the spine, and build depth there until Google treats you as the authority on that corridor. Only then do we extend to the next destinations.

Section 3 (Continued)

3. THE APPROACH · PRINCIPLES 3 & 4

3. Concentrated budget and time (80/20 Rule)

Roughly 80 percent of your results will come from about 20 percent of the work: the right keywords, the right pages, the right fixes. Our job is to find that 20 percent and put your money there instead of spreading it thin across everything.

4. Scalable foundation for growth

You've told us the plan is to expand into new routes and into other European countries over time. That changes how we build from day one. A structure designed to hold one corridor and a structure designed to add Switzerland, France and new destinations later are not the same structure. We build the second one now: a clean site architecture, proper multilingual setup, and topical authority you can extend country by country, so each new market plugs into strength you already have instead of starting from zero every time. Getting this right now is what makes expansion cheap later.
