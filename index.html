Section 2 (Continued)

BACKLINKS, LOCAL & STRENGTHS

Your backlink profile needs attention

You have 297 backlinks from 127 referring domains, but the quality is the problem. Only 12 are dofollow. The referring domains are concentrated in Finland, France, Singapore and Colombia, not Italy or Switzerland, and one anchor text appearing across 13 domains openly advertises a paid link network. This is the signature of low-quality or purchased links, and it can actively hold a site back rather than help it. It needs a proper audit and, where appropriate, a disavow.

Missing Local & Multilingual Foundations

• No LocalBusiness schema on the site, despite having a Google Business Profile in Stabio with a 5 star rating and 10 reviews. Google isn't connecting the two.
• No hreflang tags. Your clients already search in English, Italian and German, and as you expand into new countries this only grows. The site currently declares itself as en-US only, which is the wrong foundation.
• Zero AI search visibility. No mentions in ChatGPT, Gemini or Google AI Overviews.

The One Genuine Strength

Usability scores A+. The site works properly across devices, which means the foundation is sound and this is a fixable situation rather than a rebuild from zero.

Section 3

3. THE APPROACH

Four principles drive every recommendation in this document.
1. Fewer, stronger pages beat many thin ones

For Google Ads, sending a campaign to a tightly matched page makes sense and lowers your cost per click. But for organic search, a separate page for every single route works against you. Ten near-identical pages split your ranking strength ten ways and compete with each other. We give the priority routes, Milan to Como and Como to Lugano, their own strong dedicated pages, and group the rest until search demand justifies pulling them out.

2. Topical authority built around Como first

Google ranks a site higher when it sees that site as a real authority on a subject. Your site is the clearest possible example of the problem: pages cover Greek villas and Italian food, while terms you rank for are about private jets. Google has no idea you're the transfer specialist for your corridor. So we concentrate everything on Como as the hub, the Milan to Como and Como to Lugano routes as the spine, and build depth there until Google treats you as the authority. Only then do we extend to the next destinations.

3. Concentrated budget and time (80/20 Rule)

Your budget and your time are limited, so we concentrate them. Roughly 80 percent of your results will come from about 20 percent of the work: the right keywords, the right pages, the right fixes. Our job is to find that 20 percent and put your money there instead of spreading it thin across everything.

4. A foundation that scales for growth

We build a foundation that scales, because you're going to grow. You've told us the plan is to expand into new routes and into other European countries over time. A structure designed to hold one corridor and a structure designed to add Switzerland, France and new destinations later are not the same structure. We build the second one now: clean site architecture, proper multilingual setup, and topical authority you can extend country by country.
